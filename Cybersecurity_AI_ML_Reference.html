Field Update

2026 SITREP — What Changed Since The Baseline Guide

Three developments materially affect how this reference should be used in production: attackers now operationalize LLMs mid-execution, the primary US vulnerability enrichment pipeline has been rationed, and STIX 2.1/TAXII 2.1 has become the de facto CTI transport layer.

Offense

AI-Fication of the Attack Lifecycle

Google's Threat Intelligence Group reports state-sponsored and financially motivated actors now embed LLM calls inside live malware — families such as PROMPTFLUX and PROMPTSTEAL query models mid-execution to rewrite commands and evade detection, rather than using AI only in pre-attack planning. Global median dwell time has climbed to 14 days.

Vendors separately warn of "distillation attacks" against proprietary ML models, and forecast agentic, multi-step attack chains (recon → payload → lateral movement → exfil) run with reduced human-in-loop by late 2026, though UK NCSC assesses fully autonomous end-to-end attacks as unlikely before 2027.

Sources: Google Threat Intelligence Group / M-Trends 2026, Trend Micro 2026 Predictions, SecurityWeek Cyber Insights 2026

Vulnerability Data

NVD Enrichment Rationing

NIST confirmed on 15 April 2026 that CVE submission volume grew 263% between 2020–2025, and the NVD can no longer enrich every CVE with CVSS scores and affected-product data. All backlogged, unenriched CVEs published before 1 March 2026 are being moved to a "Not Scheduled" status. Going forward, NIST prioritizes CVEs affecting US federal/critical software and anything on CISA's KEV list — everything else may ship without a CVSS score.

Guide impact: pipelines in this document that assume every CVE JSON record carries a CVSS score (Vulnerability Exploitation Prediction, risk-scoring workflows) now need a fallback path — treat "Not Scheduled"/unscored CVEs as a first-class category, not a data-quality bug, and weight CNA-supplied or vendor-supplied scores higher.

Sources: NIST.gov (Apr 2026), Infosecurity Magazine, Help Net Security, Black Duck

Standards

STIX 2.1 / TAXII 2.1 Now Baseline

STIX 2.1 has displaced 2.0 as the working standard across major CTI platforms and TIPs (ThreatConnect, Anomali ThreatStream, MISP, OneFirewall). MITRE now publishes ATT&CK natively via the attack-stix-data repository in 2.1 rather than the legacy mitre/cti 2.0 set, and most TIPs support TAXII 2.1 collections out of the box as a de facto threat-intel API.

Guide impact: when building STIX ingestion in this guide's workflows, target spec_version: "2.1" and pull ATT&CK from attack-stix-data, not the older mitre/cti STIX 2.0 repo referenced in the base Quick Access Links sheet.

Sources: MITRE/CTI & attack-stix-data (GitHub), IntelHunt.ai, Kraven Security, OneFirewall CTI
